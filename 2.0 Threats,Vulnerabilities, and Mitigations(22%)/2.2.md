# Phishing

```text
Social engineering with a touch of spoofing.

Often delivered by email,text, etc

Usually not the same as the original.
```

### Business email compromise

```text
- We trust email sources
    - Attackers take advantage of this

    Spoofed email addresses - Uses emails that look like a trusted one

    Financial fraud - sends emails with updated banking information

    The recipients needs to click the links
```

### Tricks and Misdirection

```text
Digital Sleight of hand to fool us.

Typosquatting - A type of URl hijacking

Pretexting - Lying to get information
```

### Phishing with different bait

```text
Vishing - over the phone (spoof caller id)

Smishing - SMS Phishing(Trying to scam)

Variations on theme - fake check scam phone verification,
BOSS/CEo scam, advance-fee scam

etc etc think SCAMMERS!!!
```

# Impersonation

```text
Attackers pretend to be someone they aren't
They try to use details to gain your trust
Throw tons of technical details around to confuse you
```

### The Pretext

```text
- Before the attack, they state the stage.
```

### Eliciting information

```text
Tries to extract information from the victim
Often seen with vishing(voice vishing)
```

### Identity fraud

```text
Your identity may be used by others, so keep your information safe.

These can all be done in your name!!

Credit card fraud
Bank Fraud
Loan Fraud
Government benefits fraud
```

### Protecting against Impersonation

```text
- Never disclose information
- Don't disclose personal details
- Always verify before revealing information(Call 3rd party)
- verification should be encouraged when giving information
```

# BONUS!

## Watering Hole Attack

```text
If attacker can't get in they'll try to poison a website or software your organization
uses and will wait until you use it to breach into your network.

It's not limited to software/website though they can try to hack into a local coffee shop
if they know you'll be there and try to get in through that means.

The point is They preemptively will try to hack something waiting for you to get caught
there.
```

### Prevention

```text
- Defense-in-depth
    - Layered defence
- Firewalls and IPS
    - Stop the network traffic before things get based
- Anti-virus  / Anti-malware signature updates
```

# Threat Actors

```text
- The entity responsible for an event that has an impact on the safety of another entity
- Threat actor attributes
    - characteristics of the attacker
- Useful to categorize the motivation
```

---

## Attributes of threat actors

```text
Internal/ external
    - The attacker is inside the house
    - They're outside and trying to get in
Resources/ funding
    - No money
    - Extensive funding
Level of sophistication/ capability
    - blindingly runs scripts or automated vulnerability scans
    - can write their own attack malware and scripts
```

---

## Motivations of threat actors

```text
- the purpose of the attack

Motivations can be selfish or selfless, helpful to know what they want from this to know
how to proceed.
```

### Nation States

```text
- External entity
    - Government and national security
    - Many possible Motivations
        - Data exfil,philosophical, revenge,war,exactly
    - Constant attacks,massive resources
        - Called Advanced persistent threat
    Highest sophistication
        - Military control,utilities, financial control
```

### Unskilled attackers

```text
- Runs premade scripts without any knowledge of what's really happening.
- Often motivated by the hunt
- Can be internal or external
- not very sophisticated
- Not formal funding
```

### Hacktivist

```text
- A hacker with a purpose
    - Motivated by philosophy,revenge, disruption,etc.
Often an external entity
    - Could potentially infiltrate to be an insider threat

Can be remarkably smart
    - Very specific hacks
        - DoS,web site defacing,
Funding may be limited
```

### Insider Threat

```text
- Motivated by revenge or financial gain
- extensive resources
- An internal entity
- medium level of sophistication
    - real knowledge is knowing where the data might be
```

### Organized crime

```text
- Professional criminals
- Very Sophisticated
- One person hacks, one person manages exploits, another person sells the data, another
  handles customer support
```

### Shadow IT

```text
- Department working around the rules of the It department
- Limited budget
- medium sophisticated
    - May not have It training
```

# Threat Vector

```text
A method used by the attacker
    - to infect or gain access
```

---

## Message-Based Vectors

```text
- Biggest and most successful threat vector
Email:
    - Malicious links in an Email
    - Link to a malicious site

SMS:
    - Attacks in a text message

Phishing attacks use these message-base vectors very well
    - People want to click links
    - Links in an email,links send via text

Deliver the malware to the user
    - Attach it to the email
    - Scan all attachments,never launch untrusted links

Social engineering attacks
    - through Invoice scams
    - Cryptocurrency scams
```

---

## Image-Based Vectors

```text
Harder to identify the threat in an image
Some image formats can be a threat:
    SVG format
        it is an Image described in XML

Significant security concerns
    - HTML injection
    - javascript attack code

Browsers must provide input validation
    - Avoids running malicious code
```

---

## File-Based Vectors

```text
- Adobe PDF, ZIP/RAR files(any compression type), Microsoft Office
    - Contains many different files, add-in files
```

---

## Voice-Call Vectors

```text
- Vishing - Phishing over the phone
- Spam over IP - large scale phone calls
- War dialing - Attackers trying to find unpublished phone numbers
                to use.
```

---

## Removable Device Vectors

```text
- Get around the firewall by planting USBs so it will infect air gapped
  networks.

- USB devices can act as keyboards

- Data Exfiltration
```

---

## Vulnerable software vectors

```text
- Client based
    - Infected executable
    - Known vulnerabilities
    - May require constant updates

- Agentless
    - No installed executable
    - Compromised software on the server would affect all users
    - Client runs a new instance each time
```

---

## Unsupported systems Vectors

```text
- Patching is an important prevention tool
- Unsupported systems aren't patched
- outdated operating systems may not have ANY support.
```

> **Keep your inventory and records current to keep track of this**

---

## Unsecure Network Vectors

```text
The network connects everything, attackers can see any non encrypted data

- Wireless
    - Outdated security protocols (WEP,WPA,WPA2)
    - Open or rogue wireless networks

- Wired
    - Unsecure interfaces - 802.1x can be insecure

- Bluetooth
    - They can use it for reconnaissance
    - Implement vulnerabilities
```

---

## Open Service ports

```text
Most network-based services connect over TCP or UDP ports

Every open port is an opportunity for the attacker.
    - An application vulnerability or misconfiguration.

Firewall rules must allow traffic to an open port.
```

---

## Default Credentials

```text
- Most devices have a default username and passwords

The right credentials provide full control
    - admin access
Very easy to find the defaults for access points or routers
www.routepasswords.com
```

## Supply chain vectors

```text
Tamper with the underlying infrastructure

- Managed service providers(MSPs)
    - Access many different customer networks from one location
- Gain access to a networking using a vendor
    - Target credit card breach
- Suppliers
    - counterfeit networking equipment
```
