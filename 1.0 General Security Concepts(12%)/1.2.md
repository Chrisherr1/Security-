# CIA Triad

```text
Confidentiality
    - Prevent disclosure of information to unauthorized people
Integrity
    - Prevent data from being modified without knowing its been modified
Availability
    - Prevent systems and networks downtime as much as possible
```

### Confidentiality Deeper

```text
- Encryption
- Limits to Access(Access Controls)
```

### Integrity Deeper

```text
- Hashing

- Certificates

- Non-repudiation (Cannot deny what you've said)
    Eg. Signing a Contract

    Similar in Cryptography:

        We use a Hashing
        - Adds Proof of Integrity
            - Means we can verify that the data remains accurate and consistent(Didn't
              change)
            - hashing can verify that the data didn't change by comparing hashes.

        - Adds Proof of Origin, with High assurance of authenticity
            - Given where the data was given we can assume and check where it originated
            - We can also use a digital signature to make sure messages are from the
              person or find out who altered the data.
```

### Availability Deeper

```text
- Redundancy
- Fault tolerance
- Patching
```

## Authentication, Authorization, and Accounting

```text
Identification - This is who you claim to be / Username

Authentication - Prove you are who you say you are
```

### Authenticating systems

```text
How can you authenticate a device?
    Give it a digitally signed certificate on the device allowing it.
```

### Certificate authentication

```text
An organization has a trusted Certificate Authority(CA)
    - Most orgs maintain their own CAs
    - The org makes a certificate for a device and digitally signs the certificate
      with the org's CA
        - Using that the Device can now authenticate its allowed on the network.
```

### Authorization Models

```text
No Authorization Model:
    User -> Resource
        Not scalable and hard to keep track of

Authorization model:
    Abstractions
    User belongs to a Group
    Group -> Resource
    Very Scalable
```

## Zero Trust

![Zero Trust](<1.2 Digital Security ZERO TRUST.png>)

```text
Many Networks are relatively open on the inside past firewalls and security controls.

    Zero trust is a holistic approach
        - covers every device,process,person
    Everything must be verified and nothing is trusted
```

### Split the Network into Functional Planes

```text
Applies to Physical,virtual,cloud components

Data Plane - Part of device that is doing the security process(switch,router)
    - processes the frames,packets, and network data
    - processing,forwarding,trucking,encrypting,NAT

Control Plane - Manage all actions in Data Plane
    - Configuring policies or rules for a device
    - setting up routing tables,sessions, NAT tables

Data Plane vs Control Plane
    Switch -> Data Plane -> Ethernet ports, Network address settings, Configurations
    Switch -> Control Plane -> Virtual firewall
```

### Adaptive Identity - Security based on User

```text
- Look at source of the request
    User lives in US but request came from China
- Make Authentication stronger if something is odd about the User.
```

### Threat Scope Reduction - Decrease number of entry points

### Policy-driven access control - Combine the adaptive identity with a predefined set of rules

### Security Zones - Where you are connecting from and where are you connecting to

```text
You can make Trusted and Untrusted networks
- Internal network and external network
- VPN 1, VPN 5, VPN 11
- Marking,IT, Accounting,Human Resources

    You can section off zones to make security easier and harder
    Eg: Blocking Untrusted to trusted zone traffic
    Eg: Trusted to Internal Zone traffic
```

### Policy Enforcement point - Any Subjects that are communicating through the network must pass through this.

```text
Policy enforcement point
    Gate keeper
        Allow,Monitor, and terminate connections
        Can Consist of multiple components working together
```

> **VERY ABSTRACT** Just know you need a gate keeper to enforce your policies on the network
> How you do that depends on what you end up using.

### Policy Decision Point - Responsible for making a decision on whether to accept/revoke

```text
Composed of 2 parts
    1. Policy Engine
        - Evaluates each access decision based on policy and other information
        - grant,deny,revoke
    2. Policy Administrator
        - Communicates with Policy enforcement point
        - generates access tokens/credentials
        - checks the PEP to allow or disallow access
```
