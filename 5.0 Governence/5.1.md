# Security Policies

> **As a security engineer, it is our job to create:**

```text
- Create Confidentiality,Integrity,Availability.

- High level may include:
    - Data storage Requirements, security event procedures
- Specific security goals may include:
    - Appropriate Wi-Fi usage, requirements for remote access.
```

## Main point

> **Security policies are in place to answer the "what" and "why"
> As in what to do and why do we do it.**

> **IT IS YOUR JOB TO CREATE POLICIES FOR THESE**

---

## Information security policies

```text
This is a master list of all policies that should be followed for the
security of the organization.

Contains:

    Resources on what to do,Compliance Requirements,Detailed security procedures.

    Roles and responsibilities for reference.

    Its up to the org to enforce it.
```

---

## Acceptable use Policies(AUP)

```text
Covers what is a acceptable use of company assets:
    - detailed documentation
    - may be documented in the rules of Behavior

Covers many topics:
    - Internet use, telephones, computers, mobile devices,etc.

Used by an organization to limit legal liability:
    - If someone is dismissed, these are well documented reasons on why.
```

---

## Business continuity

```text
This is used to know what to do in case of a disaster or disruption to the norm.

- There needs to be an alternative to things we need.
    - Manual transactions
    - Paper receipts
    - Phone calls for transaction approvals.
```

> *This needs to be planned BEFORE using it*

---

## Disaster recovery plan

```text
Basically if it affects ALL parts of a organization.

- If a disaster happens, IT should be READY

- This is often PART of the business continuity planning.

- Meant to avoid disruptions to an organization.

Disasters vary but the important part is creating a plan for any
that might affect business processes.
```

### Comprehensive Plan

```text
- Recovery Location
- Data Recovery Method
- Application Restoration
- It team and Employees availability in said Location
```

# 5.1 Security Standards:

```text
A Formal definitions for using security technologies and processes
    - It's meant for everyone to know risks and expectations.

These may be written in house or borrowed from NIST or ISO.
```

## Passwords

```text
Organizations might set a standard for passwords.
How long and how complex.

They might also define acceptable authentication methods:
    - May Disallow local accounts.
    - Only LDAP to AD database,etc

They might also define what is allowed for password resets.
- reset frequencies,password storage, password manager options.
```

---

## Access Control

```text
Organizations might control when you're allowed to access data
and under tha circumstances.

There should also be a policies defining which access control types can be used and how
to determine how a user gets access
to a resource and how access is removed.
```

---

## Physical Security

```text
You will also need policies regarding physical security controls,
Doors,building access,etc.
    - What is being used and why.
    - How to handle firings by using mandatory escorts,offboarding,etc.

This should include how access is granted and how different employees will have access to
different things.
```

## Encryption

```text
You might want to also create policies regarding how encryption
should be used:

- Define Standards for encrypting and securing data
- include implementation standards
- Password storage
    - methods and techniques.
- Data encryption minimums
    - Algorithms for data in use, data in transit, and in rest.
    - Will probably be different for each data state.
```

# 5.1 Security Procedures:

## Change Management

```text
You should have a formal process for managing change.
This is used to avoid downtime and confusion, and mistakes.

Nothing changes without the process:
```

## Onboarding

```text
You should have a formal policy in place for new hires or transfers.

IT agreements need to be signed.

control mistakes.

IT usually is required to provide hardware. Laptops configured with the current things
outlined in the policy.
```

# 5.1 Data roles and Responsibilities

```text
1. Data Owner - High level in the org and often accountable for
                specific data, often a senior officer.

                eg: Vp of sales owns customer relationship data
                    Treasurer own the financial information.

2. Data Controller - Manages the purpose and means by which person data is processed.

3. Data Processor - Processes data on behalf of the data controller, often third part or
   different group.

Payroll controller and processor example:

    Data Controller:
    - Payroll department defines payroll amounts and time frames.

    Data Processor:
    - Payroll company processes payroll and stores employee information.


4. Data custodian/steward - Responsible for data accuracy,privacy, and security.

    - works directly with the data.
        - ensures laws and standards.
        - manages the access rights to the data
        - implements security controls.
        - Associates sensitivity labels to the data.
```

# 5.1 Risk Management

```text
It is your job to implement the policies regarding
risk identification.

It is important part of any organization.
    Growth bring risk, it is useful to get ahead of any potential problems.

Risk management, manages potential risk, qualify internal and external threats, risk
analysis helps plan for contingencies.
```

## Performing a risk assessment

```text
As a security manager it is your job to evaluate the risk of a particular action.

- The one-time the assessment may be part of one-time project, the company acquisition,
  new equipment installation, unique new security threats,etc.

- Continuous assessment
    - May be part of an existing process
    - Change controls requires a risk assessment as part of the change.
```

---

## Ad-Hoc Assessments

```text
An organization may not have a formal risk assessment process.
    - Perform an assessment when the situation requires.

CEO is back from a conference:
    - Wants to know if the organization is protected from a new attack type

A committee is created and the risk assessment proceeds.
    - Once the assessment is complete the committee is disbanded.
    - There may not be a need to investigate this specific risk again.
```

---

## Recurring assessments

```text
- The devaluation occurs on standard intervals.

An internal assessment:
    - Performed every three months at the beginning of the quarter.

A mandated risk assessment:
    - Required by certain organizations
    - Some legal requirements will mandate an assessment
    - PCI DSS requires annual risk assessments
```
